    Local executables you register by name. One ToolRequest arrives on stdin, one ToolResponse leaves on stdout. Arguments go straight to the program, never through a shell. They add observations and leave originals alone.

Personnel

Who plays over the vamp. Anyone can sit in; nobody owns the groove.

  • Library yours: the authority One local catalog, serialized by SQLite on one machine. Your originals stay where they already live.
  • Processors captions, tags, embeddings Registered executables that answer a ToolRequest with observations. Nothing they do edits the catalog directly.
  • Agents command line vamp guide prints their manual. They search, file findings with provenance, and read your ratings back.
  • Replicas the event stream Other machines follow the authority's ordered events from their last sequence onward.
  • Vampire Mac & iOS, by arrangement The library app we build on VAMP: browse, clip from the browser, replicate over Tailscale. Proprietary, and a one-time purchase.

Liner notes

Vamp till ready

Notes on VAMP 0.1

In a club, the band vamps: a figure of two or four bars, played again and again until the singer walks on or the soloist is ready. Nobody waits for anybody. The groundwork holds, and anyone can come in on the one.

VAMP is that figure for a media library. It is a small, strict agreement about what a catalog contains and how it changes, so that an app, a script, a model or an agent can join in without asking the others to stop.

Reference, not custody

VAMP never takes your files. Originals are referenced in place, and the catalog records where they are and what they are. An original is identified by the BLAKE3 digest of its exact bytes; an asset has a stable UUID that does not change when the file moves.

“Stable UUID independent of original path or byte digest.”

contract.json, identity.asset

Revisions

Every metadata change advances an asset’s revision. Write with --expected-revision and a stale write is turned away whole: no change, no event. Two hands on the same asset cannot quietly overwrite each other; the second one hears about it.

Ordered events

Each change is an event with a monotonic sequence: media_added, representation_ready, observation_added and fifteen more. The mutation, its event and any configured jobs commit in a single transaction, so nothing can happen off the record.

One authority

There is one local authority, a SQLite catalog on one machine. Replicas follow its event stream. It is not a multi-device sync protocol, and it does not pretend to be one.

Sitting in

Processors are programs you register yourself. Each receives one ToolRequest on stdin and answers with one ToolResponse on stdout: a caption, a set of tags, an embedding. They add observations; they never touch an original.

That is the whole tune. Keep time, and the rest of the band can play.
